The week started leaking into every gap I left unguarded.

A quick reply while the kettle boiled, one more message before walking inside, a half-read email just before bed. None of it looked dramatic, but by Wednesday I could feel the cost. My breath had crept higher into my chest. My shoulders stayed faintly prepared. Even when nothing urgent was happening, part of me was still braced for the next small demand.

What unsettled me most was not the volume of work. It was what constant availability was doing to my attention. I answered faster, but with less depth. I moved things along, but noticed less about what actually needed care.

The boundary that helped was almost embarrassingly small. Once I closed the laptop, I stopped checking messages for the rest of the evening. Not forever, not as a grand rule, just as a clean end point for that part of the day.

The result was not instant calm. It was something more useful. A longer exhale. More contact with the room I was actually in. Less residue waiting for me the next morning before the day had even started.

Sometimes the boundary that saves a week is simply the point where continuation stops.
