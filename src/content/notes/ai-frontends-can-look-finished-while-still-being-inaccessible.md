An AI frontend can look finished long before it is actually usable.

I keep seeing flows that render clean screens, polished components and credible copy, but the first keyboard pass breaks the illusion. Focus disappears. Labels do not connect. Error states are vague. The product looks ready in a screenshot and starts failing the moment a real person has to move through it.

AI can help teams get to visible surface area faster, but speed at the surface can hide what is missing underneath. When a team is under pressure, that visual progress is easy to trust.

Then the cost arrives later, in handover friction, support noise, rework and the quiet loss of trust that comes when a product feels good in review but rough in use.

This is why I do not think accessibility is a clean-up pass at the end. It is part of software finish-line judgement. It tells you whether the thing only looks complete or whether it holds up with keyboard use, assistive technology and ordinary human variation.

The build is not finished because the screenshot is convincing. It is finished when the interface can still keep its shape under real use.
