At the end of a workday, I closed the laptop and realised my shoulders had not finished working.

Nothing dramatic had happened. The calls were over, the decisions made, and the room was quiet. But when I stood up, my shoulders were still lifted and my breath was sitting higher than usual, as if another request might arrive next.

I noticed it when I began my Tai Chi and dantian breathing practice. The movement was familiar, but my lower breath was not fully available. Part of me was leaning into the day, still holding unfinished tensions accumulated across messages, choices and changes of direction.

This is one reason embodied practice matters to my working life. It gives me information that thought alone can miss. I may believe I handled the pressure well, yet the body can show that I have been carrying the work through effort rather than centre.

That observation is not a judgement. If I finish the day braced, the question is not only how to relax afterwards. It is what in the work keeps asking for that posture.

Sometimes the answer is pace. Sometimes it is an unclear handoff, a decision without enough ownership, or the habit of staying available to every small demand.

Practice does not remove the work. It helps me see more honestly how I am doing it, and whether the way I am carrying it is sustainable.
